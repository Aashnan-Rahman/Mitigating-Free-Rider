\chapter{Conclusion}

This thesis investigated whether a server can mitigate free riding using only returned model updates, without trusting client-reported accuracy, loss, training time, or resource use and without training an auxiliary detector. SWT-CP v8 addresses this objective through secret trap models, two-sided norm and layer-profile evidence, a candidate/suspicion state machine, repeated probing, rotating anchors, cumulative penalties, and rehabilitation.

The IID evaluation is encouraging across both MNIST and CIFAR-10: every tested free rider was removed at attacker shares of 30\% and 40\%, and the best-observed CIFAR-10 IID precision is 98.36\% at 30\% and 100\% at 40\%. FR1--FR3 also retain strong recall under non-IID partitioning. These results support the use of unpredictable server interventions and longitudinal evidence for lightweight detection of replay, random-update, and historical-average behavior.

The evaluation also reveals limits that are as important as the successes. Non-IID false positives concentrate on particular honest clients and recur across attacks, showing that joint statistical evidence remains sensitive to legitimate client heterogeneity. This is especially visible in the new CIFAR-10 30\% batch: all attackers are removed, but individual non-IID runs also remove 7--18 honest clients. Earlier MNIST FR4 runs additionally exhibit a contamination transition in which undetected attackers enter the anchor roster and malicious-majority panels contribute to an honest removal. Rotation is therefore not equivalent to trust establishment.

The immediate research priority is to replace negative anchor eligibility (``never flagged'') with positive, repeated validation, and to use differential responses from the same client across independent traps so that each client partly serves as its own non-IID control. Additional seeds and Dirichlet concentrations should test whether the CIFAR-10 false-positive rate persists. Broader validation should include client dropout, on--off and partial-training free riders, collusion, defense-aware adaptation, and comparisons with implemented baselines. A secure protocol is also needed if per-client auditing is to coexist with secure aggregation.

SWT-CP should thus be understood as a computationally simple and empirically strong detector within a defined operating region, not as proof that a client trained. Its main contribution is both constructive and diagnostic: it demonstrates where secret statistical traps work, and identifies the precise combination of heterogeneity, adaptive fabrication, and contaminated references under which they fail.
