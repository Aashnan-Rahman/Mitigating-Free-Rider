3 seeds is common in federated learning papers, and it's often all the compute allows. Your CIFAR-10 runs take about 2.5 to 3 hours each, so I understand the limit. I'd still expect a reviewer to push on it, for these reasons:

- **The claims are about rare events.** "100% recall" and "99.91% precision" depend on a handful of honest removals (3 in total). Table II shows that honest quarantines vary a lot between seeds, for example [1-9] on CIFAR-10 non-IID FR3 at 40%. With so much spread, 3 seeds can't tell you whether the true honest-removal rate is near zero or a few percent.
- **The pooled numbers overstate the sample.** The 99.91% comes from roughly 3,360 removals, but those are not independent. They come from only 96 runs, and they share partitions and seeds.
- **No uncertainty is shown.** Even with 3 seeds, the main tables report means without standard deviations or intervals.

A cheaper fix than the 5-10 seeds I suggested:

1. Run **5 seeds** on the configurations that carry your claims: non-IID at 30% and 40%, for FR3 and FR4. These are the only ones with honest errors. FR1 and FR2 are removed at the earliest round every time, so they can stay at 3.
2. Report **mean ± std** in the tables, and a Wilson or Clopper-Pearson interval for the honest-removal rate, counted per honest client.
3. If you keep 3 seeds, drop "100%" and "99.91%" as headline numbers, report per-run results, and state in the paper that the seed count is small. That is weaker, but it won't read as overclaiming.

The honest-only (0% free rider) runs and the 5-10% free-rider runs matter more than extra seeds on the settings you already have.
