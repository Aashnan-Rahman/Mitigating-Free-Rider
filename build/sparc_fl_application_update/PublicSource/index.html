About

My academic interests lie in trustworthy federated learning, computer security, adversarial machine learning, and privacy in distributed systems. I am particularly interested in lightweight defenses that remain practical under real computational and communication constraints.

Alongside research, I teach foundational courses to undergraduate students and advise students working in computer security and federated learning.

Current research

Master’s thesis2024—Present

SPARC-FL: Statistical Peer Auditing with Repeated Confirmation for Free-Rider Detection in Federated Learning

SPARC-FL compares client update norms and layer profiles in scheduled peer audits and requires repeated evidence before removal. Its 96 challenge-dispatch runs removed all tested free riders, with three honest-client removals. In 16 matched runs at 40% free riders, ordinary dispatch achieved the same removals while withholding fewer honest updates.
